<h2>Overview</h2>
To optimize the testing pipeline at the Energy Materials Lab, I engineered a custom multi-channel printed circuit board (PCB) designed to significantly accelerate device characterization. By enabling simultaneous 8-pixel measurement, this hardware solution reduced overall characterization time by nearly 80% while maintaining the stringent signal integrity required for high-precision, low-noise data collection.

<h2>Hardware Design & Layout Optimization</h2>
The board was designed from the ground up in KiCAD, balancing complex electrical and physical constraints.

<ul>
    <li>Physical Integration: I optimized the layout by incorporating specific rectangular apertures into the board design to prevent optical shading during testing.</li>

<li>Hardware Compatibility: To bridge the gap between new capabilities and existing lab infrastructure, I engineered custom interfaces that provided direct, drop-in connectivity with legacy measurement hardware, ensuring seamless software and hardware compatibility without requiring systemic overhauls.</li></ul>

<h2>Manufacturing & Validation</h2>
Beyond the digital layout, I managed the end-to-end hardware realization process. This included overseeing the full PCB fabrication and assembly cycle with external vendors, conducting rigorous Design for Manufacturability (DFM) reviews, and designing a custom mechanical holder to secure the setup.

Once manufactured, I led the physical hardware verification. Utilizing oscilloscopes and standard multimeters, I systematically debugged the test fixtures to minimize contact resistance, ensuring high reliability and repeatable precision across continuous measurement cycles.
